# E214 — NVFP4 fraction reversal across prompt, layer, head and timestep

Mode: user-requested completeness/exploration. Problem: determine prevalence and location of the E142/E143 anomaly under fixed 10% INT8 and increasing NVFP4. Hypothesis: a subset of heads/layers exhibit larger video-query output NMSE when more previously skipped tiles are computed in NVFP4, with recurrence across timesteps and prompts. Decision: whether head/layer selective treatment and subsequent scale diagnosis are warranted.

Minimum valid setup: real BF16 dense closed-loop H3 QKV, four historically paired prompts 25,7,48,44, fixed seeds, 480×832 124 frames, 20 steps, all 50 main layers and 56 heads. Identical input and BF16 reference for three native DraftAttention2 Q64 arms, skip/NVFP4/INT8 = 80/10/10, 50/40/10, 0/90/10; official per-layer NVFP4 scales, original DraftMap maxpool weight 0.2, dense INT8 prefix. Percentages are global video tiles, converting to retained fractions 0.5/0.5, 0.8/0.2, 0.9/0.1. Video-query output NMSE against BF16 same-input reference, per head and aggregate. Record numerator/denominator and no threshold strict flags for any of 10→40, 40→90, 10→90; additionally record effect size to distinguish tiny effects. All 20 steps, all layers, all 4 prompts; no causality claim for individual tiles. Maintain dense teacher trajectory regardless of compared arms. Invalid cheap proxy: synthetic QKV or comparing different generated trajectories. A measured reversal only shows this whole-policy comparison, not an isolated tile-level FP4-versus-skip proof. Prompt 44 (swimmer) is inferred as the fourth from the E202 four-case set; all subjects checked against prompt_id.

Expected outcomes: widespread, concentrated or absent reversals trigger respectively precision-allocation study, targeted head analysis, or de-prioritization. Necessary evidence: 4×20×50×56 per-head paired results and completion checks. Supporting: head/layer/time/prompt summaries, effect size, deterministic replay/sanity. Stop condition: invalid routing, missing/nonfinite observations, inconsistent capture contract, memory/resource failure; record and report instead of inferring. No closed-loop video-quality or causal scale claim.
